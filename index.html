Why IRL, why now

The internet is filling up with machines. The room is where you can still tell who's real.

51%

of all web traffic in 2024 was automated: the first time in a decade that bots outnumbered people.

Source: 2025 Imperva Bad Bot Report (Thales)

~50%

of new articles published online are now mostly written by AI.

Source: Graphite study of 65,000 web articles, reported by The Conversation

34×

more successful: a request made face to face, compared with the same request by email.

Source: Harvard Business Review, A Face-to-Face Request Is 34 Times More Successful Than an Email

When anything on a screen can be generated, the signals that still mean something are physical: who showed up, who you sat next to, who vouched for you in the room. That's the bet behind the Operator Network, and the subject of our podcast, The Belonging Economy.

The rooms

Small on purpose.

No sponsors buying seats, no badge scanning, no pitches from the stage. One table, one topic or one thing being built, and enough time to actually talk.

Dinners

A short guest list around one table. Founders next to the investors and operators who back companies like theirs.

Demo nights

Working products, not slides. What Stockholm's builders shipped, shown live to people who know what good looks like.

Breakfasts

CEO, CFO and CMO round tables before the workday starts. One question, about an hour, under the Chatham House rule.

Build days

Engineers, product leads and bank partners building side by side, from breakfast to demos. See the last one.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Operator Network?

A Stockholm executive tech network for people in SaaS, AI and fintech: founders, C-suite executives, operators and investors. It meets in real life, in small invite-only rooms such as dinners, demo nights, breakfasts and build days. It is run by Alexandra Grisanti.

How do I join the Operator Network?

There's no membership and no membership fee. You apply to individual events: pick one on the Insider Events page and apply on Luma, ideally two weeks ahead. The host approves every seat to keep each room the right size and mix. To hear about new events first, follow the Operator Network calendar on Luma.

Is it a SaaS executive network, an AI network or a fintech network?

All three, on purpose. Most of the people in the network lead or build SaaS, AI and fintech companies in Stockholm, and the hardest problems in each now cross into the others. Read more about the SaaS and AI executive network.

Why meet in person when AI can do so much online?

Because online signals are getting cheap. In 2024 automated traffic passed human traffic on the web for the first time in a decade, and around half of new articles online are now mostly AI-written. Showing up, sitting at the same table and being vouched for in person are still hard to fake. The full case is on Why IRL.

What does it cost?

Nothing. There's no membership fee. Most rooms are free to attend; a few carry a small commitment fee, such as 100 kr for a breakfast round table, which is shown on the event's Luma page.

What is The Belonging Economy?

The Operator Network's podcast and newsletter about life after AI and why people are meeting in real life again. It will be recorded in Stockholm with speakers from our events. About the podcast.

Is there a membership or a membership fee?

No. There's no membership to apply for and no membership fee. You apply to individual events on Luma, and the host approves every seat.

My company wants to host a room or get visibility. Where do I go?

To Alexandra Studio, the B2B growth studio run by the same founder. The Operator Network doesn't sell sponsorship, seats or agenda time; companies that want a room with their buyers host it through Alexandra Studio, under their own name. Contact Alexandra.
