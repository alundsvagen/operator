Why meeting in real life is the future after AI

AI made content, outreach and even conversation almost free to produce. That's exactly why the room is getting more valuable. The case behind the Operator Network, in plain numbers.

By Alexandra Grisanti Published 11 November 2026 6 min read

The short answer

Because trust needs proof, and online proof is getting cheap. In 2024 automated traffic passed human traffic on the web for the first time in a decade, and around half of new articles online are now mostly written by AI. When anything on a screen can be generated, the signals that still carry weight are physical: who showed up, who sat at the table, and who vouched for you in person.

So the people who run companies are moving their most important relationships offline, into small rooms. That's what the Operator Network does in Stockholm, and what our podcast, The Belonging Economy, is about.

The internet is filling up with machines

Two numbers explain most of it.

  • 51% of all web traffic in 2024 was automated, the first time in a decade that bots outnumbered people online, according to the 2025 Imperva Bad Bot Report from Thales. Malicious bots alone made up 37%.
  • Around half of new articles published online are now mostly AI-generated, according to a Graphite study of 65,000 web articles. The share climbed quickly after ChatGPT launched and has hovered around 50% since late 2024.

None of this means AI content is useless. It means that content, by itself, has stopped being a signal. A polished post, a well-written cold email or a confident deck used to suggest that someone had done the work. Now it suggests that someone had access to a model.

Trust moves to where it can be checked

When a signal gets cheap to fake, people look for one that isn't. For relationships, that's presence.

  • A request made face to face is 34 times more successful than the same request sent by email, in research by Vanessa Bohns and Mahdi Roghanizad summarised in Harvard Business Review.
  • Event organisers rank face-to-face networking conversations as the element that builds the most trust: 72% put it first in PCMA Convene's 2026 research.
  • In Cvent's 2026 research, 81% of event marketers said trust in their organisation increased after customers attended an in-person event, and more than a quarter named generic or AI-generated content as the biggest risk to audience trust.

The pattern is consistent: the more of the online world that can be generated, the more weight people put on what they saw and heard in person.

What changes for operators

Your feed is no longer proof

Posting consistently still matters, but it no longer separates you from anyone with a model and a schedule. What separates you is that people in your market have met you, and would say so.

Warm introductions become the channel

When inboxes fill with AI-written outreach, the introduction from someone a buyer, investor or candidate trusts is the message that still gets opened. Those introductions come from rooms.

Small rooms beat big stages

At a conference booth you get ten or twenty minutes with someone who is also being sold to. Around one table you get about two hours. That difference is the whole reason the Operator Network keeps its rooms small.

Showing beats describing

A demo working live, or a prototype built in a day with a bank's engineers at the table, is hard to fake. That's why our formats include demo nights and build days, not just talks.

The belonging economy

We use the belonging economy for the part of business that runs on being present, trusted and remembered by a group of people. It grows as AI makes everything else cheaper to produce. It's the subject of our podcast, which we'll record in Stockholm with the speakers from our events.

How we run rooms for a post-AI world

  • Small on purpose. Dinners with a short guest list, round tables of 8 to 25, demo nights and build days with mixed teams.
  • Every seat approved. Guests request a seat on Luma and the host approves each one, so the table is worth sitting at.
  • No paid seats. The Operator Network doesn't sell sponsorship, seats or agenda time. See Partners.
  • Something real at the centre. One question, one live demo or one thing being built, rather than a keynote.
  • Off the record where it counts. Round tables run under the Chatham House rule. See how round tables work.

Questions people ask

Why are in-person events growing after AI?

Because AI has made online signals cheap. Automated traffic passed human traffic on the web in 2024, and about half of new articles are mostly AI-written. Being present in a room, and being vouched for by people who were there, is still hard to fake, so trust is moving offline.

Is AI making networking less valuable?

It's making online networking less distinctive. AI can write the connection request and the follow-up. It can't sit at the table. The introductions and relationships that come from meeting in person are worth more, not less.

What is the belonging economy?

The part of business that runs on being present, trusted and remembered by a group of people. It grows as AI makes content and outreach cheaper. It's also the name of the Operator Network's podcast.

Where can I meet SaaS and AI executives in person in Stockholm?

At Operator Network events: invite-only dinners, demo nights, breakfasts and build days for SaaS, AI and fintech executives, operators and investors. See Insider Events.
