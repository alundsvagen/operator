Stockholm fintech

A fintech leadership community in Stockholm

Who the Operator Network brings together from Stockholm's fintech scene, what happens in its rooms, which topics have come up so far, and how to apply. And what it isn't: a place to buy access to executives.

By Alexandra Grisanti Published 25 October 2026 7 min read

The short answer

The Operator Network is a Stockholm community for people who lead fintech, AI and SaaS companies, or are about to: founders, CFOs, CTOs, CMOs, heads of product and partnerships, and senior technical experts. It has more than 100 members and a wider network of more than 200 people in Stockholm, and it meets in person, in small rooms: round table breakfasts, dinners for 8–12 people, and the occasional build day.

Joining is by application and free so far. No company can sponsor a seat, so the people at the table are there as peers, not as prospects.

Who is in the room?

Much of Stockholm's fintech work now runs through software, data and partnerships, so the network deliberately mixes fintech with AI and SaaS. The people at the table tend to be:

  • Founders and CEOs of fintechs, from seed stage to scale-up
  • CFOs and finance leads, who carry reporting, funding and growth decisions and rarely get to compare notes with peers
  • CTOs, engineering leads and technical experts building on banking and payments infrastructure
  • CMOs, heads of growth and heads of partnerships, who have to reach banks and other licensed buyers
  • Product leaders working on embedded finance, payments, lending and compliance

People in the network have worked at companies such as Klarna, Tink, Visa, Worldline and Froda. Jurors, mentors and discussion leads at recent rooms came from Danske Bank, Worldline, Froda, Open Payments, Zwapgrid, Gilion and Fairlo.

Who can apply

Experience
At least two years of professional experience
Role
CEO, CTO, CFO or CMO, a manager position, or a technical expert position. Responsibility matters more than title.
Sector
AI, fintech or SaaS
Location
Living in Stockholm, because everything happens in person
Size today
More than 100 members, in a wider network of 200+ people (October 2026)
Cost
Free so far. Paid membership is planned at 850 kr a year and hasn't started.

The criteria exist so that everyone at the table works in the same world and carries comparable weight. That is what makes people willing to talk honestly about the hard parts of the job.

What happens in the network

Round tables

One topic, one table

Role-based breakfasts, such as a CFO round table or a CMO breakfast. One topic, about an hour of discussion, led by a few participants rather than a speaker. How the round tables work.

Dinners

8–12 peers

Curated tables of senior people, under the Chatham House rule. No vendors, no pitches, time to actually talk.

Build days

Hands on real APIs

Vendor-neutral hackathons for technical leaders, like the Embedded Finance Build Day, where 44 builders in 15 teams worked on real banking APIs.

Members also get stages: speaker invitations and panel seats, discussion-lead roles, and editorial features in the Stockholm Growth & Fintech newsletter, which 193 people at 53 banks read. Features are never sold.

What the network is not

  • Not a sponsored event series. No company pays for seats, introductions or agenda time. Until October 2026 the network offered event sponsorship; it no longer does, and the partners page explains why.
  • Not a sales channel. Nobody pitches. Companies that want a room with specific buyers work with Alexandra Studio, and those events run under the company's own name, so guests always know who is hosting.
  • Not a conference. Rooms are small on purpose. The value is in a long conversation, not a long guest list.

How to join

  1. Apply. The application takes a few minutes: who you are, what you run, where you're headed.
  2. Review. Every application is reviewed for experience, role, sector and fit. Not everyone gets a yes.
  3. Meet. If it's a fit, Alexandra books a meeting with you.
  4. Join the rooms. You start receiving invitations. When paid membership launches, you'll be told before anything is invoiced.

Questions people ask

Is there a community for fintech leaders in Stockholm?

Yes. The Operator Network is a Stockholm community for people who lead fintech, AI and SaaS companies, or are about to: founders, CFOs, CTOs, CMOs, heads of product and partnerships, and senior technical experts. It meets in person in small rooms, such as round table breakfasts, dinners and build days. Joining is by application.

Who can join the Operator Network?

People with at least two years of professional experience; a CEO, CTO, CFO or CMO role, a manager position or a technical expert position; working in AI, fintech or SaaS; and living in Stockholm. You don't need to be C-suite. Every application is reviewed.

How big is the Operator Network?

More than 100 members, in a wider network of more than 200 people in Stockholm, as of October 2026. Rooms are kept small on purpose: 8 to 12 people at a dinner and up to about 25 at a breakfast round table.

Does it cost anything to join?

Not yet. Paid membership is planned at 850 kr a year, with a three-month trial before the first invoice, and has not started. Some rooms carry a small commitment fee, such as 100 kr for a breakfast round table.

Can a company sponsor or pitch at an Operator Network room?

No. The Operator Network does not sell sponsorship, seats, introductions or agenda time, and nobody pitches. Companies that want to meet specific buyers work with Alexandra Studio, and those events run under the company's own name.

Is the Operator Network only for fintech?

No. It brings together people in AI, fintech and SaaS. Fintech is at the centre of many of its rooms, from CFO round tables to the Embedded Finance Build Day, but members from AI and SaaS sit at the same tables.
